# Cross-lab failure patterns

## P1: state and knowledge are easy to conflate

Protocol learners initially map “no ACK” to “receiver failed.” Home learners map “router” to “gateway.” Audio learners can map a changed playback/control setting to a changed analysis model. The fix is causal labeling: **system state**, **observed evidence**, **learner conclusion**.

## P1: vocabulary arrives after the phenomenon

This is pedagogically productive, but the bridge is inconsistent. Terms that caused hesitation: sampling phase, Nyquist, folded frequency, primary reading, carry versus overflow, accepted/delivered/ACK observed, tick, fixture, provenance, nullable, offending byte, and index base.

## P2: prediction is available but optional

Impatient and curious learners often Step or Run first. This is not a failure of the causal engine; it weakens the research/teaching loop. Teacher prompts can compensate now. A shared lightweight prediction checkpoint would improve evidence without forcing a long quiz.

## P2: evidence controls are not always self-explanatory

Repeated reports mention sliders or phase controls that did not visibly commit, inspection buttons whose effect was unclear, and selected historical evidence displayed beside final output. Verify with a human browser before implementation; some reports may reflect automation interaction.

## P2: reset, preset, and history semantics blur

Image and Byte learners questioned whether Reset/Original restored the baseline or appended a new evidence frame. A stable distinction is needed: **restore baseline**, **choose fixture**, **apply edit**, **clear history**.

## P3: dense bilingual/technical surfaces

Long tables and mixed Chinese/English labels did not block transfer, but increased hesitation. Use shorter labels plus a one-line definition at the point of use.
